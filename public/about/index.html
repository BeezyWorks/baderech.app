About

Shalom, I'm Mattaniah.

ותן חלקנו בתורתך

I'm a software developer, and I build these apps in my spare time. I build them because I wanted them and couldn't find them: a siddur that doesn't make me flip around on Rosh Chodesh, a Chumash with real mefarshim I can open on the bus, and Mishnayos that remembers where I left off.

It started in 2014 with an Android siddur called Ice Cream Siddur. Back then I didn't know how to connect a button to the code. That siddur grew into Siddur One, and Siddur One was rewritten every time I learned a better way to build it. In 2026 it got two siblings, Mikros BaDerech and Mishnayos, and the family got a name.

Why "Baderech"?

Twice a day in Shema we say וְדִבַּרְתָּ בָּם בְּשִׁבְתְּךָ בְּבֵיתֶךָ וּבְלֶכְתְּךָ בַדֶּרֶךְ: you should speak words of Torah when you sit at home and when you walk on the way. For a lot of us, most of the day happens baderech. We're commuting, waiting, between one thing and the next. These apps are for those minutes.

Why they're free

This isn't a business, and I'd like to keep it that way. There are no ads, no subscriptions, no paywalled perakim, and no selling of anyone's data. If the apps help you, the best things you can do are use them, tell a chavrusa, and send me a note when something's wrong. If you want to help with the hosting bills, there's a Ko-fi page, and every bit helps.

Getting it right

A siddur that shows Tachanun on the wrong day isn't a small bug. I take the halachic details seriously, and the texts and mefarshim come from established sources like Sefaria, with credit given inside the apps. Even so, I'm one person, and mistakes happen. If you see a wrong word, a missing minhag or something that doesn't look right al pi halacha, please tell me. Those reports are the most useful thing I get.
